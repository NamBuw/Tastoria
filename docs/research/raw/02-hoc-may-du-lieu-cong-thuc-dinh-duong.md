## 3. Interaction datasets

- **Food.com** ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shuyangli94/food-com-recipes-and-user-interactions), [paper](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1613/)).
  - License: "Data files © Original Authors". Total 892 MB, last updated 2019-11-08.
  - Files: RAW_recipes.csv 295 MB, RAW_interactions.csv 349 MB, PP_recipes 205 MB, PP_users 14 MB, interactions_train/validation/test 28.2/0.29/0.52 MB, ingr_map.pkl 0.9 MB.
  - The paper reports **230K+ recipes and 1M+ reviews, 2000–2018**. Filtered: 180K+ recipes and 700K+ reviews; train has 25,076 users, 160,901 recipes, 698,901 actions.
  - Split: each user's latest review is test, the second-latest is validation. **Dev/test recipes are disjoint from training recipes** ("We evaluate only on recipes not in the training set"), so pure collaborative filtering cannot score the official test set.
  - **The design doc's "270K recipes / 1.4M interactions" is wrong.** Raw counts (231,637 / 1,132,367 / 226,570 users) are UNVERIFIED.
- **RecipeNLG** ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.inlg-1.4/), [HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mbien/recipe_nlg), [official site](https://recipenlg.cs.put.poznan.pl/)).
  - 2,231,142 recipes with fields title, ingredients, directions, link, source (Gathered/Recipe1M+) and an NER food-entity field.
  - Terms: "only for non-commercial research and educational purposes."
  - CSV is 2.14 GB ([Kaggle mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/paultimothymooney/recipenlg) shows 2.31 GB in total).
  - It has no user interactions.
- **MealRec+** (SIGIR 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05386), [GitHub](https://github.com/WUT-IDEA/MealRecPlus)).
  - Built from Allrecipes. Each meal is appetizer + main + dessert, and **meals are simulated** from user–course sessions.
  - Two variants: H (1,575 users, 3,817 meals, 46,767 user–meal interactions) and L (1,928 users, 11,807 user–meal interactions).
  - Includes FSA/WHO health scores and an 8:1:1 split. The predecessor is [MealRec 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12133).
- **Cookpad Japan via NII IDR** ([page](https://www.nii.ac.jp/dsc/idr/cookpad/)): 1.72M recipes plus menus labelled main/side, up to 2014-09; 1.8 GB 7z. Academic institutions only, with a sealed application. Impractical for this project.
- **ViFoodRec** ratings (§1a) are the only Vietnamese option I found, and the imputed median values make them unreliable as ground truth.

**Evaluation practice**
- **Sampled metrics:** [Krichene & Rendle, KDD 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3394486.3403226) show sampled metrics can reverse which of two models looks better and collapse toward AUC. Avoid them; with 150–200 dishes, rank the full catalog.
- **Splitting:** [Meng et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13237) show the split strategy alone changes model rankings. [Ji et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11060) show leave-last-one-out leaks future data, and recommend splitting on a global timeline.
- **Baselines:** [Dacrema et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06902) could reproduce only 7 of 18 published models, and 6 of those were often beaten by simple kNN or graph heuristics. [Cremonesi et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1145/1864708.1864721): models that minimize RMSE do not necessarily do well on top-N (verified via Semantic Scholar TLDR). The specific claim that TopPop is a strong baseline in that paper is UNVERIFIED this session.
- **BPR tuning:** [Revisiting BPR (RecSys 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3640457.3688073) found implementation differences cost up to 50% of performance, and that a tuned BPR comes close to state-of-the-art results.

## 5. Ingredient substitution

| Work | Key verified points |
|---|---|
| [Teng, Lin, Adamic 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2380718.2380757) | Substitute network built from user modification suggestions on Allrecipes; complement network from co-occurrence |
| [Pellegrini et al., HEALTHINF 2021](https://doi.org/10.5220/0010202000670077) | Food2Vec and FoodBERT, plus multimodal variants. Ground-truth and human evaluation. Multimodal FoodBERT was best. |
| [Shirai et al. 2021 (FoodKG)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7861309/) | DIISH = embeddings + KG + food-category filtering. Eval sets: Cook's Thesaurus (2,360 pairs), Food.com reviews (3,313). Metrics MAP, MRR, RR@k. On Food.com: Word2Vec MRR 0.326 / RR@5 0.460; DIISH 0.373 / 0.519. |
| [Fatemi et al. 2023 (arXiv), GISMo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07960), [code and data](https://github.com/facebookresearch/gismo) | **Recipe1MSubs**: 49,044 / 10,729 / 10,747 substitution tuples (train/val/test) mined from comments on Recipe1M recipe pages. **CC-BY-NC**; pickle files about 6 MB in total. Metrics MRR and Hit@1/3/10. **Hit@3: Food2Vec 10.7, lookup-table + frequency 32.3, GISMo 35.9.** |
| [Senath et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04922) | Mistral-7B fine-tuned (SFT + DPO) on Recipe1MSubs: Hit@1 22.04 |
| [Achananuparp & Weber 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08807) | Distributional similarity on MyFitnessPal food diaries, judged by crowdworkers |

**Implication.** Pure co-occurrence embeddings perform poorly for finding substitutes. A lookup table of known pairs, ranked by how often each pair appears, is about 3× better, and filtering by food group helps further.

## 6. Meal and menu composition under constraints

- [Elsweiler & Harvey 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2792838.2799665): tests whether daily meal plans for sampled user profiles, using real preference data, can meet nutrition guidelines (abstract withheld by the publisher; summary from Semantic Scholar TLDR). The doc's "phần lớn hồ sơ" claim is not independently checked.
- [Trattner & Elsweiler, WWW 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3038912.3052573) and their [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02760): post-filtering for health trades away accuracy.
- [Toledo et al., IEEE Access 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2929413): AHPSort pre-filter, then an optimization stage for daily plans. Evaluated by case study.
- Bundle recommendation: [BGCN (SIGIR 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03475), [CrossCBR (KDD 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00242). Evaluated with Recall/NDCG@K.
- **MealRec+ hard vs soft constraints:** a hard filter (FSA ≤ 8) dropped Recall@20 from **0.375 to 0.068**. Re-weighting kept **0.282** and still improved the FSA score from 9.32 to 8.37. Report preference metrics and constraint satisfaction side by side.

## Recommended plan

**Recipe knowledge base (150–200 dishes)**
1. Build the seed list from NIN dish names plus monngonmoingay's Canh/Xào/Kho/Luộc categories. Use ViFoodRec only as a read-only frequency reference.
2. Take gram amounts from two or more sites per dish. Normalize spoons/trái/nhánh with a unit table. Record whether each weight is as-purchased or edible portion. Rewrite the steps in the team's own words.

**Nutrition table**
1. Parse the FAO-hosted 2007 PDF into CSV: TCVN3→Unicode conversion, 526 foods, keep the refuse %. Spot-check about 5% against the NIN online lookup.
2. Fall back to USDA Foundation / SR Legacy (CC0, small files) only when a food is missing.
3. Validate the calculation pipeline on 30–50 NIN dishes whose images list ingredient grams. Look these up by hand, consistent with the doc's "no bulk download" rule.

**Ranking experiments**
1. Re-split Food.com RAW_interactions yourselves: a global time cutoff as the main split, leave-last-out as a secondary. Treat rating ≥4 as positive, rank the full catalog, and report bootstrap CIs by user.
2. Baselines: MostPop, ItemKNN, BPR (Cornac or implicit), LightFM, and LightGBM lambdarank as the re-ranker.
3. Use the official interactions_test.csv only for a cold-start (hybrid model) comparison.
4. Run LightFM in a **Python 3.11 conda-forge environment**. Do not use RecBole on 3.12.

**Meal-tray composition**
1. Keep the 10³ enumeration, but expand k when no feasible tray exists, or switch to OR-Tools CP-SAT.
2. Report both feasibility rates (ingredients, E_mâm ± δ) and preference metrics.

**Substitution**
1. Build a Vietnamese lookup table of substitution pairs, curated from cooking guides and "thay bằng" phrases in recipes, and rank by frequency.
2. Use Word2Vec (gensim, trained on RecipeNLG NER lists plus Vietnamese ingredient lists) only to re-rank or extend coverage, restricted to the same food group.
3. Evaluate on **≥100** Vietnamese pairs with Hit@1/3/10 and MRR (GISMo protocol). Baselines: random within group, frequency, lookup table.
4. Optionally check the method on Recipe1MSubs first (non-commercial license; load its pickle files only in a sandbox).

## Risks and unknowns

- **Copyright.** monngonmoingay forbids reuse without consent. ViFoodRec has no license and was scraped from it. Cookpad blocks AI crawlers. Cooky no longer appears to host recipes.
- **NIN tools.** The APIs are undocumented with no stated terms. The basis of dish portions exists only in images. Which table edition sits behind the 853 online entries is unknown.
- **FCT 2007.** About 20 years old, legacy encoding, license unstated. Bui 2025 data is not public and has no meat or fish.
- **NIN 2026.** The full document was not found. The 0.93 g/kg and free-sugar figures are unverified, and the doc's PALs do not match NIN's.
- **FoodKeeper.** Schema and Vietnamese produce coverage are unverified; scripted downloads get 403.
- **Library maintenance.** LightFM is unmaintained, RecBole does not install on 3.12, and the Microsoft Recommenders cornac<3 pin conflicts with cornac 3.x.
- **Doc errors.** The Food.com counts are wrong, and the official Food.com test split contains only cold-start recipes.
- **Ingredient mapping.** Coverage of RecipeNLG→Vietnamese ingredient mapping (rau muống, mồng tơi, mắm…) is unknown and is likely low for vegetables.
- **Sample sizes.** With n=50 substitution pairs and Hit@3 ≈ 0.4, the 95% CI is about ±0.14 (±0.10 at n=100). The survey of 80–100 people limits power.
- **Coverage.** The search budget ran out partway, so other Vietnamese datasets may exist that I did not find.
